How this calculator works

It follows the standard order landlords and lenders use. Start with scheduled income: units times monthly rent, plus any other income. Subtract a vacancy and credit loss allowance to get effective income, the rent you can realistically expect to collect.

Then subtract operating expenses: property taxes and insurance (entered per year, divided by 12), owner-paid utilities, HOA and other monthly costs, plus three percentage-based lines. Maintenance and CapEx reserves are a percentage of scheduled rent; property management is a percentage of collected income, since most managers are paid on what they collect. What remains is net operating income (NOI). Subtract the mortgage payment to get cash flow. If you enter the cash you put in, cash-on-cash return is annual cash flow divided by that amount.

The default numbers describe a fictional duplex: two units at $1,450, 5% vacancy, a $1,650 mortgage, $4,200 a year in taxes, $1,800 in insurance, 5% each for maintenance and CapEx, 8% management, and $120 of owner-paid water.

On a quick "rent minus mortgage" check, this duplex looks like it clears $1,250 a month. Once taxes, insurance, vacancy and honest reserves are counted, it roughly breaks even. Self-manage (management 0%) and it turns positive, at about $195 a month. That gap between the napkin math and the real math is what this calculator is for.

Choosing your percentages

There is no universal right number, and published rules of thumb vary. Many small landlords budget something like 5–10% of rent each for maintenance and CapEx, more for older buildings or properties with original systems. Property managers often charge a percentage of collected rent, frequently somewhere around 8–10%, plus leasing fees. If you have a year or two of your own records, use those instead. Your actual repair spending is a far better guide than any rule.

What this doesn't include

Income tax, depreciation, principal paydown, appreciation, and one-time costs like a major renovation are left out on purpose. This is a monthly cash picture, not an investment return model or a tax estimate.

FAQ

What vacancy rate should I use?

Use your own history if you have it: months empty divided by total months across your units. Without history, many small landlords budget somewhere around 5–8%, more for older properties, student areas, or markets where units sit longer. One empty month a year is about 8% of rent.

Why are maintenance and CapEx separate?

Maintenance covers recurring small repairs: a faucet, a filter, a service call. CapEx (capital expenditures) covers big items that wear out on a schedule: roof, water heater, HVAC, flooring. Setting money aside monthly for both keeps one bad year from wiping out your cash flow.

Is NOI the same as cash flow?

No. Net operating income (NOI) is income minus operating expenses before the mortgage. Cash flow is what is left after the mortgage payment. NOI describes the property; cash flow describes your loan and the property together.

Does this calculator include CapEx reserves in NOI?

Yes. Here NOI is after your maintenance and CapEx reserves, which is the more conservative view. Some lenders and appraisers calculate NOI before reserves. If you want that version, set CapEx to 0% and add it back mentally.

What should I put in cash invested?

Everything you paid out of pocket to buy and get the property rentable: down payment, closing costs, and initial repairs or rehab. Leave it at 0 to skip cash-on-cash return.

Does this include income tax or depreciation?

No. It is a pre-tax cash estimate. Depreciation, deductions and your tax situation are questions for a tax professional.
