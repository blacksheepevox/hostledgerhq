How this calculator works

First it counts the calendar days the tenant has the unit that month. For a move-in that is the move-in day through the last day of the month; for a move-out it is the 1st through the move-out day. Both ends count. Then it sets a daily rate. With the actual-days method the daily rate is the monthly rent divided by the days in that month (28 or 29 in February, 30 or 31 otherwise), so a full month always adds back up to one month of rent. With the 30-day method the daily rate is the monthly rent divided by 30 in every month, and the days charged are capped at 30. The prorated rent is the daily rate times the days charged.

When landlords prorate rent

The common cases are a move-in that does not land on the 1st, a move-out mid-month at the end of a lease or under an early-termination agreement, and a switch of the rent due date (for example from the 15th to the 1st) where the gap month is billed as a partial. Some landlords skip proration and start the lease on the 1st or the 15th instead. Whatever you choose, putting the method and the first payment in the lease avoids an argument later.

FAQ

Which proration method is correct: actual days or a 30-day month?

Neither is universally correct. Many landlords divide the monthly rent by the actual number of days in that month, so every day costs exactly its share of that month. Others use a 30-day “banker's month” so the daily rate is the same all year. If your lease or local law names a method, use that one. If not, pick one, put it in writing, and use it the same way for every tenant.

How do I prorate rent for a move-in vs a move-out?

For a move-in, count from the move-in day through the last day of the month, including the move-in day. For a move-out, count from the 1st through the move-out day, including it. Then multiply those days by the daily rate. Some leases count the move-out day differently, so check yours.

Should I collect prorated rent and the first full month at move-in?

Many landlords collect the prorated partial month and a security deposit at move-in, then the first full month on the 1st. Some move-ins late in the month collect the partial month plus the next full month together. What you can collect up front, and how large a deposit can be, may be limited by state or local law.

Does the law require me to prorate rent?

It depends on where the property is and what the lease says. Some places set rules for move-out or early termination; many leave it to the lease. This calculator applies no legal rules or caps; it only does the arithmetic. Check your lease, your state and local rules, or an attorney.

Why does the 30-day method give a different answer in February or a 31-day month?

The 30-day method uses the same daily rate (rent ÷ 30) in every month, so a 15-day stay costs the same in February as in March. The actual-days method charges more per day in February (28 or 29 days) and less in 31-day months. Under the 30-day method this calculator never charges more than 30 days, so a full calendar month is never more than one month of rent.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
