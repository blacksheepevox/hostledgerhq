How this calculator works

Break-even nightly rate is what you need so that, after the platform takes its percentage and you pay your per-stay costs, the nights you expect to book cover your fixed monthly costs. Divide fixed costs by nights booked, add the per-stay cost spread over the average stay, then divide by what you keep after the fee (1 − fee %). The target rate does the same with your target monthly profit added to fixed costs. Break-even occupancy flips the question: at the nightly rate you enter, how many of the available nights must be booked so contribution per night covers fixed costs. Monthly revenue and profit use your entered rate and expected nights. Fixed costs, per-stay costs, target profit and the nightly rate are clamped at zero; stay length is at least one night; the platform fee is clamped between 0 and 100% (at 100% the rates are blank).

A worked example

The default numbers describe a fictional cabin: $2,310 a month in fixed costs, $90 paid per turnover, a 3% platform fee, 3-night average stays, 20 nights booked out of 30 available, a planned $180 nightly rate, and a $970 target monthly profit.

So $150 a night covers costs at 20 booked nights; $200 a night would clear the $970 profit target; at $180 you need about 53.3% occupancy (roughly 16 booked nights) just to break even. Zero nights booked leaves the rate fields blank; a 100% platform fee does too.

When hosts use a break-even rate

New listings need a floor before guessing at market ADR. Returning hosts recompute it when the mortgage, insurance or cleaner rate changes, or when average stay length shortens and turnovers pile up. Run it once per season: shoulder months with fewer booked nights push the break-even rate up even if costs stay the same. Pair it with the short-term rental profit calculator when you want the full month (cleaning fees charged, occupancy tax remittance, expense detail).

FAQ

What is a break-even nightly rate?

It is the average nightly rate you need so that, after the platform fee and what you pay per stay (cleaning, supplies), the nights you expect to book cover your fixed monthly costs. Above it you make money at that occupancy; below it you lose money. It does not include income tax, occupancy tax you remit yourself, or one-off repairs.

How is this different from the STR profit calculator?

The profit calculator starts from a nightly rate and occupancy and shows monthly net profit and break-even occupancy. This page starts from your costs and expected nights and answers how much to charge: break-even rate, rate for a target profit, and break-even occupancy at a rate you enter. Use both when you are setting or reviewing rates.

Should I include a cleaning fee guests pay?

This calculator models lodging only. If you charge a separate cleaning fee, use the Airbnb cleaning fee calculator for that fee, and keep per-stay costs here as what you actually pay the cleaner and for supplies. Or bake cleaning into the nightly rate and leave cost per stay as zero.

What platform fee percentage should I use?

Use the host fee percentage your payout report actually shows, not a generic published rate. For direct bookings, enter your card-processing percentage, or 0. The fee must be under 100% or the break-even rate is blank.

What if break-even occupancy says not reachable?

At the nightly rate you entered, each booked night loses money after the platform fee and per-stay costs, so no occupancy covers fixed costs. Raise the rate, cut per-stay costs, or shorten average stays (fewer turnovers) before chasing more bookings.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
