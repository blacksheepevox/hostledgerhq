How this calculator works

Cost per turnover is what you pay the cleaner (a flat amount, or hourly rate times hours) plus laundry or linens plus supplies. Because the booking platform usually takes its percentage out of the cleaning fee too, the fee that exactly covers your cost is a bit higher than the cost: break-even fee = cost ÷ (1 − platform fee %). Your monthly cleaning cost is the cost per turnover times the number of stays. If you enter the fee you charge now, the calculator shows how much you keep after the platform fee and whether that is over or under your cost each month.

A worked example

The default numbers describe a fictional one-bedroom: a cleaner paid $70 a turnover, $20 of laundry, $10 of supplies, a 3% platform fee, eight stays a month, and a $95 cleaning fee.

Worked example
Cost per turnover ($70 + $20 + $10)$100.00
Break-even fee ($100 ÷ 0.97)$103.09
Monthly cleaning cost (8 × $100)$800.00
Kept from a $95 fee ($95 × 0.97)$92.15
Short per stay ($92.15 − $100)−$7.85
Short per month (8 × $7.85)−$62.80

A $95 fee looks like it covers a $70 cleaner. Add laundry, supplies and the platform's cut and this host pays about $63 a month out of the nightly rate to clean. Paying the cleaner $28 an hour for 2.5 hours gives the same $70; switch the pay option to hourly to try it.

Costs that are easy to leave out

  • Linen and towel replacement. Sheets and towels wear out. Add a little per turnover to laundry so replacements are covered.
  • Restocking. Coffee, toilet paper, soap and trash bags are per-stay costs, not one-off purchases.
  • Deep cleans. A few times a year the place needs more than a turnover. Spread that cost across the stays in between.
  • Your own time. If you do turnovers yourself, put a value on the hours. It shows what you would pay to hand it off.

Using the result when you set prices

A cleaning fee is a fixed charge per stay, so it weighs more on a one-night booking than on a week-long one. Some hosts price the fee at break-even, some charge less and raise the nightly rate, and some set a minimum stay. The calculator does not pick for you. It tells you what a turnover costs, so the choice is deliberate. To see how cleaning fits into the whole month, try the short-term rental profit calculator.

FAQ

Does the platform take a fee from the cleaning fee?

Booking platforms generally calculate their fee on the booking subtotal, which usually includes the cleaning fee. How much comes out of your payout depends on your fee setup, so use the percentage your payout report shows. For direct bookings, enter your card processing percentage, or 0.

Should my cleaning fee equal my cleaning cost?

That is a pricing choice. Some hosts charge the full cost, some charge less and build the rest into the nightly rate, and some use a minimum stay so one cleaning is spread over more nights. Whatever you choose, it helps to know the break-even number first, so you know what you are giving up.

What if I clean the place myself?

Enter an hourly rate for your own time to see what a turnover is really worth, or enter 0 for the cleaner to see only the out-of-pocket cost of laundry and supplies. Looking at both is useful when you decide whether to hire help.

Is occupancy tax charged on cleaning fees?

In many places it is, but the rules vary by city, county and state. If your platform collects and remits the tax, it does not change this calculation. If you remit it yourself, check your local rules.

Why per turnover and not per night?

A cleaning happens once per stay, whether the guest stays one night or seven. That is why short stays cost more in cleaning per night, and why the number of stays per month drives your monthly cleaning bill.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
