How this calculator works

Available nights are the nights in the period minus nights you blocked for your own use or for maintenance. Occupancy rate is booked nights divided by available nights. ADR is rental revenue divided by booked nights, and RevPAR is rental revenue divided by available nights. For the whole year, the calculator adds up the nights and revenue first and then divides, so a short month counts less than a long one.

A worked example

The default numbers describe a fictional month: 30 nights, 4 blocked for the owner's own stay, 19 booked nights, and $3,420 of rental revenue.

Worked example
Available nights (30 − 4)26
Occupancy (19 ÷ 26)73.1%
ADR ($3,420 ÷ 19)$180.00
RevPAR ($3,420 ÷ 26)$131.54
Occupancy counting all 30 nights63.3%

The same month reads as 73% or 63% depending on whether the owner's nights count. Neither is wrong, but mixing the two between months makes trends meaningless. Pick one and stick to it.

Occupancy, ADR and RevPAR together

Occupancy alone can mislead. Dropping your rate fills more nights but may earn less; raising it earns more per night but may leave gaps. RevPAR combines the two: it is what each night you offered earned on average, booked or not. If a pricing change raises RevPAR, it probably helped, even if occupancy fell.

Booked nights are not stays

Count paid nights, not bookings. Ten one-night stays and two five-night stays are both ten booked nights, but they are very different months for cleaning costs. To see what turnovers cost you, try the Airbnb cleaning fee calculator.

FAQ

Should owner-blocked nights count as available?

Most hosts leave them out. Nights you blocked for your own use or for maintenance were never for sale, so counting them as empty makes occupancy look worse than your pricing really performed. This calculator shows both versions so you can compare: occupancy of available nights, and occupancy counting every night in the period.

What is the difference between ADR and RevPAR?

ADR (average daily rate) is rental revenue divided by booked nights: what a booked night earned on average. RevPAR (revenue per available night) is rental revenue divided by available nights, booked or not. RevPAR equals ADR times occupancy, so it shows whether a higher rate cost you more empty nights than it was worth.

Should cleaning fees be included in revenue?

For ADR and RevPAR, use nightly charges only and leave out cleaning fees and taxes. That keeps the numbers comparable with your nightly rates. Whatever you choose, be consistent from month to month.

Is a booked night the same as a stay?

No. A stay is one booking; a booked night is one paid night. A three-night stay is three booked nights. Occupancy rate counts nights.

What is a good occupancy rate?

There is no single good number. It depends on your market, the season and how you price. Compare a month with the same month last year, and use the break-even occupancy from the short-term rental profit calculator to see what your costs require.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
