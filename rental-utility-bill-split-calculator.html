How this calculator works

First it takes the total bill and the number of units (1–10). Then it builds a weight for each unit: equal gives every door the same weight; by occupants uses the people living in each unit; by square footage uses floor area; blended mixes the two with the occupants weight you set (the rest is sq ft). Each unit’s bill share is its weight over the total weight, times the bill. Shares are rounded to cents with a largest-remainder step so they still add up exactly to the bill. An optional admin fee is then added per unit on top of the utility share. Total recovered is the allocated bill plus all admin fees.

When landlords use RUBS or a utility split

Master meters are common in small multifamily buildings where separate meters were never installed. Landlords then need a written way to pass the shared bill through to tenants: equal shares when units are similar, occupant or square-footage ratios when they are not, and sometimes a small admin fee for the billing work. Whatever you use should match the lease the tenant signed and the rules where the property sits. Keep the master bill, the allocation method and each unit’s charge together so a dispute later has a paper trail.

Local rules come first. RUBS and utility billing rules vary by state and city. Some places restrict or ban ratio utility billing, require submeters for new work, or limit what you can add as a fee. This page is not legal advice; confirm the rules for your property and put the method in the lease before you bill.

FAQ

What is RUBS (ratio utility billing system)?

RUBS is a way some landlords allocate a master-metered utility bill across units using a ratio: equal shares, number of occupants, square footage, or a blend of those. Each tenant pays their computed share (sometimes plus a small admin fee) instead of having a separate meter. Whether RUBS is allowed, and how it must be disclosed, depends on state and city rules and on the lease.

How should I split utilities between units?

Common methods are an equal split (same amount per door), by occupants living in each unit, by square footage, or a blended weight (for example 50% occupants and 50% square footage). Pick one method, put it in the lease, and use it the same way every billing period. This calculator runs the arithmetic; it does not choose the legal method for your property.

Do I need separate meters?

Submeters measure each unit’s actual use and bill that use. RUBS estimates a share of a master bill when submeters are not installed. Some jurisdictions encourage or require submeters for new construction, and some restrict or ban RUBS. Check local law before you choose.

Can I add an admin or billing fee?

Some landlords add a small per-unit fee to cover the cost of allocating and billing the master meter. Whether that fee is allowed, how large it can be, and whether it must be in the lease varies by place. Enter it here only if your lease and local rules allow it.

Why don&rsquo;t the cents always divide evenly?

A bill in cents rarely divides into equal whole-cent shares. This calculator rounds each unit’s share to the nearest cent and uses a largest-remainder step so the shares still add up exactly to the bill. That way the allocation reconciles and nobody is short or long a penny.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
