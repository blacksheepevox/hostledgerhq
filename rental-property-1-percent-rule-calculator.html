How this calculator works

Actual % is monthly gross rent divided by purchase price, times 100. Annual rent is monthly times 12. If purchase price is zero, actual % is blank. The optional target % (default 1) decides pass/fail, the monthly rent needed at this price, the max purchase price that still meets the rule at this rent, and the monthly gap vs that rule rent (positive means above the rule). A target of zero or less blanks those reverse fields. Negatives are clamped to zero. The calculator does not model vacancy, expenses, mortgages or taxes.

When investors use the 1% rule

Buyers use the 1% rule (or a local variant) to filter listings before they dig into expenses. Listing sheets and investor forums still quote it as a shorthand for rent relative to price. Owners recompute it when rents change after a lease renewal or a vacancy fill. It is a screen, not a full underwrite: two properties with the same actual % can still diverge once vacancy, operating costs, deferred maintenance and financing enter the picture. In expensive markets many solid deals never clear 1%; in cheaper markets clearing 1% is common and not automatically “good.”

FAQ

What is the 1% rule in real estate?

The 1% rule is a rough screen: monthly gross rent should be at least about 1% of the purchase price. A $350,000 property collecting $2,450 a month is at about 0.70%—below the classic 1% screen. Some investors use 0.7% or 2% instead; this calculator lets you set the target. The rule ignores vacancy, expenses, CapEx, financing and taxes.

How is the 1% rule different from GRM or cap rate?

The 1% rule compares monthly rent to price. GRM compares price to annual rent (they are inverses of each other when scaled). Cap rate uses net operating income after vacancy and operating expenses. Use the 1% rule or GRM as a quick gross-rent screen; use cap rate and cash flow when you have a rent roll and expense history.

Does a property that fails the 1% rule always make a bad investment?

No. Many solid rentals in higher-priced markets screen below 1%. The rule is a filter, not a verdict. Local rents, expenses, appreciation, financing and your own underwriting matter more than any national rule of thumb.

Should I use scheduled rent or collected rent?

Either works if you stay consistent across deals. Scheduled/contract rent is common for a listing screen; trailing collected rent is more conservative. This calculator takes monthly gross rent and multiplies by 12 for the annual figure.

What target % should I use?

Classic teaching uses 1%. Some markets never clear 1%, so investors use 0.7% or similar as a local screen; others want 1.5% or 2% in cheaper markets. Enter whatever target you want to test. A target of zero blanks the reverse fields (rent needed, max price, gap, pass/fail).

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
