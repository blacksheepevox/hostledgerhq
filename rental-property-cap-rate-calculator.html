How this calculator works

Cap rate is annual NOI divided by purchase price, as a percent. If the price is zero, the rate is blank. The optional target cap runs two reverses: the purchase price that would produce that rate at this NOI (“value at target cap”), and the annual NOI you would need at this price to hit the target. Negatives are clamped to zero. The calculator does not model mortgages, tax brackets or appreciation.

When landlords use cap rate

Buyers use cap rate to compare listings on an apples-to-apples, unlevered basis before they layer in a mortgage. Sellers and brokers quote it as a shorthand for income relative to price. Owners recompute it when NOI changes after a rent increase, a vacancy stretch or a big CapEx project. It is a screen, not a full underwrite: two properties with the same cap can still diverge once financing, deferred maintenance and local rent trends enter the picture.

FAQ

What is cap rate?

Cap rate (capitalization rate) is annual net operating income divided by the property’s purchase price (or current value), shown as a percent. A $350,000 property with $24,500 of annual NOI has a 7% cap rate. Higher cap rates mean more income relative to price; they can also signal higher risk or a softer market. Cap rate ignores how you finance the deal.

Is NOI the same as cash flow?

No. NOI is income after vacancy and operating expenses, before mortgage debt service, income tax and depreciation. Cash flow subtracts the mortgage (and other financing) from NOI. Two buyers of the same property can share one cap rate and still have very different cash-on-cash returns once leverage differs.

Should I use list price or what I actually pay?

Use the purchase price you expect to close at (or the current market value if you already own it). Cap rate on an asking price is a quick screen; the rate that matters for your deal is on the price you actually pay.

What is a &ldquo;good&rdquo; cap rate?

There is no universal number. Cap rates vary by market, property type, condition and interest rates. Compare a deal to similar local rentals rather than to a national average. This calculator only does the arithmetic; it does not say whether a rate is attractive.

Does this include mortgage, taxes or appreciation?

No. Cap rate is unlevered: it ignores financing, income tax, depreciation schedules, CapEx timing and appreciation. For a levered view, use the rental property cash flow calculator. For tax basis and 27.5-year depreciation, use the depreciation calculator.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
