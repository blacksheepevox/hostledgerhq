How this calculator works

It models one average month. Booked nights are your nights available times your occupancy rate. Dividing booked nights by your average stay length gives the number of stays, which matters because cleaning fees and cleaning costs happen once per stay, not once per night.

Revenue is booked nights times your nightly rate, plus the cleaning fee you charge on each stay. Expenses are the platform fee (a percentage of that revenue), occupancy tax if you remit it yourself, what you pay your cleaner per turnover, and your fixed monthly costs. Net profit is revenue minus expenses, before income tax.

A worked example

The default numbers describe a fictional cabin: $180 a night, 65% booked over 30 nights, 3-night average stays, a $95 cleaning fee, $85 paid to the cleaner, a 3% platform fee, and $2,320 a month in fixed costs.

What break-even occupancy tells you

Every booked night earns its rate minus the platform fee and any occupancy tax you remit. Each stay also adds the cleaning fee you charge minus what you pay the cleaner, spread over the nights in that stay. Divide your fixed monthly costs by what one booked night contributes and you get the number of nights you need each month just to cover the mortgage, utilities and the rest. As a share of nights available, that is your break-even occupancy.

If break-even is close to the occupancy you actually get, the property has little cushion. Shorter average stays push break-even up, because you pay for more turnovers. If the result says "not reachable", each booked night loses money after fees and cleaning. Fix the per-night economics first.

Mistakes that make the numbers look better than they are

  • Treating the cleaning fee as profit. Most of it usually goes to the cleaner and laundry. Enter both sides.
  • Guessing the platform fee. Fee structures differ by channel and setup: a low single-digit percentage on a split-fee arrangement, much more on a host-only fee. Use the number from your payout report.
  • Using peak-season occupancy all year. Run the calculator for your low, mid and peak months separately.
  • Forgetting occupancy tax you remit yourself. If you collect it inside your rate and pay it later, it is not your money.
  • Leaving out irregular costs. Replacing linens, a broken appliance and furniture wear show up eventually. Put a monthly allowance into "Other fixed".

FAQ

What occupancy rate should I use?

Use your own history if you have it: booked nights divided by nights available for the same month last year. If you are new, look at the month you are planning, not an annual average. Shoulder months and peak months can differ a lot, so run the calculator once per season.

Should the platform fee apply to the cleaning fee?

In this calculator, yes. Channels typically calculate their fee on the booking subtotal, which usually includes the cleaning fee you charge. Use the percentage your payout report actually shows.

How does the occupancy tax setting work?

If your booking platform collects and remits occupancy tax for you, choose that option and tax has no effect on your net. If you remit it yourself out of what guests pay you, choose the second option and enter your rate. The calculator then deducts that percentage of nightly revenue plus cleaning fees. Rules on what is taxable vary by city, county and state, so check your local rules.

Is break-even occupancy the same as profit?

No. Break-even occupancy is the share of nights you need booked so revenue, after fees, occupancy tax and cleaning, covers your fixed monthly costs. Above it you make money; below it you lose money. It does not include income tax, depreciation, or one-off repairs.

Does this include income tax or depreciation?

No. It is a cash estimate before income tax. Depreciation, deductions and your tax situation are questions for a tax professional.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
