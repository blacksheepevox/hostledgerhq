How this calculator works

Total deductions are unpaid rent plus cleaning or make-ready plus repairs beyond normal wear plus any other allowed line you enter. The balance is the deposit you still hold, plus any interest you owe the tenant, minus those deductions. If the balance is positive, that is the refund due. If deductions exceed the deposit plus interest, the refund is zero and the leftover is the amount still owed by the tenant. The calculator does not decide what is legal to withhold; it only does the arithmetic on the numbers you type.

If the same unit also had $800 of unpaid rent and $400 more in repairs, deductions would jump to $1,460 and the refund would fall to $40. Push repairs and cleaning high enough and the balance flips: the tenant owes the difference instead of getting money back.

What often counts as a deduction vs normal wear

At a high level, many landlords treat unpaid rent, unpaid utilities the tenant was responsible for, and documented damage beyond ordinary living as candidate deductions. Gradual wear from normal use (faded paint, worn carpet paths, loose door stops) is usually not. Cleaning that brings the unit back to the condition required by the lease can be a gray area. None of that is a rule you can rely on from this page: the lease and your state or city set the line, and an attorney or local landlord association is the right place to check it.

Why a deposit log beats a spreadsheet cell

A one-line cell that says “$1,500 held” does not show when it was received, which unit, what you already deducted, when you sent the refund, or which receipt backs each line. FAQ

Do I have to pay interest on a security deposit?

Some states and cities require landlords to hold deposits in an interest-bearing account and pay the interest to the tenant. Others do not. This calculator has an interest field so you can include it when it applies; it does not look up your jurisdiction. Check your state and local rules, or ask an attorney.

What is normal wear and tear?

Normal wear is the gradual aging of a place from ordinary living: scuffed paint, worn carpet in high-traffic spots, faded curtains. Damage beyond that (a hole in the wall, a burned countertop, missing appliances) is often treated differently. The line depends on the lease and local law, so this page does not draw it for you.

Can unpaid rent come out of the deposit?

Many leases and jurisdictions allow unpaid rent to be deducted from a security deposit, often alongside documented damage and cleaning beyond ordinary wear. Whether and how that works where you are is a legal question. Enter the amount here only if you are allowed to withhold it.

How soon do I have to refund the deposit?

Refund deadlines are set by state and sometimes local law, and they are often short (commonly a few weeks after move-out, sometimes with a written itemization). Missing a deadline can carry penalties. This calculator has no countdown; check your jurisdiction.

What if I have several units?

Run the calculator once per move-out. Each tenancy has its own deposit, deductions and deadlines. A deposit log that tracks every door separately keeps the numbers from mixing.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
