How this calculator works

Business miles are your rental trips per month times the round-trip miles per trip, times the number of months, plus any one-off trips you enter separately. The mileage amount is those miles times the standard mileage rate. If the rate changed during your period, the regular miles for the months before the change use the earlier rate and the rest (plus one-off trips) use the current rate. Parking fees and tolls for business trips are added on top, because they are generally not included in the per-mile rate.

About the 2026 mileage rate

The IRS publishes a standard mileage rate for business use of a car. For tax year 2026 it changed mid-year: 72.5 cents a mile for January 1 – June 30, 2026, and 76 cents a mile for July 1 – December 31, 2026 (source: the IRS standard mileage rates page, checked October 9, 2026). The calculator defaults to the current 76-cent rate, with the first six months of the year at 72.5 cents. This page does not update itself when the IRS announces a new rate, so confirm the rate for your tax year and type it in. For a past year, put that year's rate in the current-rate box and set the earlier-rate months to 0.

Which miles usually count

Driving that is part of running the rental: showing a vacancy, meeting a plumber, doing a move-out inspection, buying supplies for the property, handling a turnover. Personal stops on the same trip do not count. Whether a given drive qualifies, including the drive from your home to the property, depends on your circumstances, so ask a tax professional if you are not sure.

Standard rate or actual expenses

Instead of the per-mile rate, you can generally deduct the business share of the car's actual costs: gas, insurance, repairs and depreciation. There are rules about when each method can be used and about switching between them. This calculator only does the standard-rate method. Your tax professional can tell you which one fits.

FAQ

What mileage rate should I use?

The IRS standard mileage rate for business use for the tax year and dates you are working on. For 2026 the IRS changed the rate mid-year: 72.5 cents a mile for miles driven January 1 to June 30, 2026, and 76 cents a mile from July 1 to December 31, 2026. This calculator starts at 76 cents with the first 6 months at 72.5 cents. Rates change, so confirm the rate for your tax year on the IRS standard mileage rates page and type it in.

Why are there two rates?

The IRS raised the 2026 business rate from 72.5 to 76 cents a mile effective July 1, 2026. Miles driven before July 1 use the old rate and miles from July 1 use the new one. Set “Months at an earlier rate” to the number of months in your period that fall before July 1, or to 0 to use one rate for everything (for example, a different tax year).

Can I add parking and tolls?

Generally, yes. Business parking fees and tolls are usually added on top of the standard mileage amount, which is why they are a separate input. Keep the receipts. Parking tickets and fines do not count.

Which trips count as rental business miles?

Trips you make to run the rental, such as showing a vacant unit, meeting a contractor, doing an inspection, or picking up supplies for the property, are the usual examples. Personal errands do not count. Whether a particular drive qualifies, including the drive from your home, depends on your situation, so ask a tax professional if you are unsure.

Standard mileage rate or actual car expenses?

You can generally choose between the standard mileage rate and the business share of your actual car costs (gas, insurance, repairs, depreciation). There are rules about when you can use or switch methods. This calculator only does the standard-rate method; a tax professional can tell you which method fits you.

Does this work for short-term rentals too?

The math is the same for any rental: miles times the rate, plus parking and tolls. How a short-term rental is treated for tax purposes can differ from a long-term rental, so check with a tax professional how it applies to you.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
