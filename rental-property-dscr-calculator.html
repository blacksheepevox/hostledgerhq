How this calculator works

DSCR is annual NOI divided by annual debt service. If debt service is zero, the ratio is blank. Negative NOI produces a negative DSCR (that is intentional). The optional target DSCR runs two reverses: the NOI you would need at this debt service, and the maximum annual debt service this NOI can support at the target. Debt service and the target are clamped at zero; NOI is not. The calculator does not build an amortization schedule, tax return or lender worksheet.

A worked example

The default numbers describe a fictional rental with $24,500 of annual NOI and $18,000 of annual debt service (about $1,500 per month), with a 1.25× target DSCR.

To hit a 1.25× target at $18,000 of debt you need $22,500 of NOI; at the current $24,500 NOI you can support at most $19,600 of annual debt service and still clear 1.25×. Zero debt service leaves DSCR blank; a zero target leaves the reverse fields blank.

When landlords use DSCR

Buyers check DSCR before they lock a loan amount or down payment. Owners recompute it when rent, vacancy or the mortgage payment changes, or when a refinance quote arrives. Cap rate screens the property unlevered; cash-on-cash screens your cash return after debt; DSCR screens whether the property’s income covers the loan. It is a lender screen, not a full underwrite: two properties with the same DSCR can still diverge once reserves, personal income and exit plans enter the picture.

FAQ

What is DSCR?

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) is annual net operating income (NOI) divided by annual debt service. A property with $24,500 of NOI and $18,000 of annual mortgage payments has a DSCR of about 1.36×. Lenders use it to see whether the property’s income covers the loan payments with a cushion.

Why do lenders care about DSCR?

Most rental loans require a minimum DSCR (often around 1.20–1.25, sometimes higher). Below the floor, the deal may need a larger down payment, a lower loan amount, or a different product. The calculator does not replace a lender’s underwriting worksheet.

What counts as debt service?

Usually the annual principal and interest (P&I) on the qualifying loan—often 12 × the monthly payment. Some lenders add property taxes, insurance or HOA into a PITIA figure. Enter the debt service your lender will use; this page does not build an amortization schedule.

What counts as NOI?

NOI is income after vacancy and operating expenses, before debt service, income tax and depreciation. Lenders may haircut rents, use market rents, or exclude certain owner-paid items. Confirm the NOI definition with your lender rather than copying a listing flyer.

What is a &ldquo;good&rdquo; DSCR?

Many conventional rental programs look for about 1.25× or higher, but floors vary by lender, product and market. A DSCR under 1.0× means NOI does not cover debt service. This calculator only does the arithmetic.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
