How this calculator works

First it finds the days past grace: days late minus the grace period, never below zero. A flat fee or a percent of rent is charged only when that number is greater than zero (the payment is past the grace period). A daily fee is the daily amount times the days past grace. If you enter an optional cap, the late fee is limited to that amount. The total due is the monthly rent plus the (possibly capped) late fee. The calculator does not apply state or local late-fee caps on its own.

When landlords use a late fee

A written late fee is meant to recover the cost of chasing a late payment and to give tenants a clear reason to pay on time. It only works when the lease spells out the method, the grace period and any cap, and when local law allows that fee. Charging more than the lease or the law allows can create a bigger problem than the late rent. Keep a record of the due date, the day you received payment and the fee you assessed so the rent roll matches what the tenant was told.

FAQ

How do landlords usually charge a late fee?

Three common lease patterns are a one-time flat dollar amount once rent is past the grace period, a one-time percentage of the monthly rent, or a daily fee for each day past the grace period. Some leases combine a flat fee with a daily fee; this calculator runs one method at a time. Whatever you charge should match the lease the tenant signed.

What is a grace period?

A grace period is the number of days after the rent due date before a late fee starts. If rent is due on the 1st and the lease gives a 3-day grace period, a payment on the 4th is still on time for the fee, and a payment on the 5th is one day past grace. Enter 0 if your lease has no grace period.

Do states cap late fees?

Many states and some cities limit how large a late fee can be (for example a flat dollar cap, a percentage of rent, or a rule that the fee must be “reasonable”). Some places also regulate grace periods or when you can serve a pay-or-quit notice. This calculator applies only the optional cap you type in; it does not encode any state or local statute. Check your lease and jurisdiction, or an attorney, before you charge a fee.

Can I charge a late fee that is not in the lease?

Usually no. Late fees are typically enforceable only when the amount or formula is written in the lease the tenant agreed to. Putting the method (flat, percent or daily), the grace period and any cap in the lease avoids an argument later.

Is the late fee part of &ldquo;rent&rdquo; for eviction or deposit purposes?

It depends on the lease language and local law. Some places treat unpaid late fees differently from unpaid rent. This calculator only adds the fee to the rent figure you entered so you can see a total due; it is not a legal characterization.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
