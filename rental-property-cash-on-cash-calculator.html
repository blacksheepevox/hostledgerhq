How this calculator works

Cash-on-cash return is annual pre-tax cash flow divided by cash invested, as a percent. If cash invested is zero, the rate is blank. Negative cash flow produces a negative CoC (that is intentional). The optional target return runs two reverses: the annual cash flow you would need at this invested amount, and the maximum cash you can invest at this cash flow and still hit the target. Cash invested and the target are clamped at zero; cash flow is not. The calculator does not build a financing schedule, tax return or appreciation model.

When landlords use cash-on-cash

Buyers use CoC to compare levered deals after they know the down payment and the mortgage payment. Cap rate screens the property; CoC screens the deal as financed. Owners recompute it when cash flow changes after a rent increase, a rate reset or a big CapEx project funded from pocket. It is a screen, not a full underwrite: two properties with the same CoC can still diverge once tax treatment, deferred maintenance and exit plans enter the picture.

FAQ

What is cash-on-cash return?

Cash-on-cash (CoC) return is annual pre-tax cash flow divided by the cash you put into the deal, as a percent. A property that returns $6,400 a year on $80,000 of cash invested has an 8% cash-on-cash return. It is a levered metric: it reflects your down payment, closing costs and rehab cash, and the cash flow left after debt service.

How is cash-on-cash different from cap rate?

Cap rate is unlevered: annual NOI divided by purchase price (or value), with no mortgage in the picture. Cash-on-cash uses the cash you actually put in and the cash flow after debt. Two buyers of the same property can share one cap rate and still have very different CoC returns once leverage and cash invested differ.

What counts as cash invested?

Usually the down payment, closing costs you pay in cash, and any rehab or reserve cash you fund at purchase. Do not count the financed portion of the purchase price. If you later put in more cash (a cash-out refinance is the opposite), update the invested figure so CoC stays honest.

What does a negative cash-on-cash mean?

Annual cash flow is negative: the property is costing you money each year after the mortgage and operating expenses. That can be intentional (a value-add during rehab) or a red flag. The calculator shows the negative percent so you can see the size of the gap.

What is a &ldquo;good&rdquo; cash-on-cash return?

There is no universal number. Targets vary by market, risk tolerance, interest rates and whether you expect appreciation or tax benefits to make up the rest. Compare a deal to your own hurdle rate and to similar local rentals. This calculator only does the arithmetic.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
