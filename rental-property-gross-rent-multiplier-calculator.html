How this calculator works

Gross rent multiplier (GRM) is purchase price divided by annual gross rent. Annual rent is monthly gross rent times 12. If annual rent is zero, GRM is blank. The optional target GRM runs two reverses: the purchase price that would produce that multiplier at this rent (“price at target GRM”), and the monthly rent you would need at this price to hit the target. Negatives are clamped to zero. The calculator does not model vacancy, expenses, mortgages or taxes.

The default numbers describe a fictional $350,000 rental collecting $2,450 a month ($29,400 a year) with a target GRM of 10.

So this example screens at a GRM of 11.90. To buy it at a GRM of 10 you would need the price closer to $294k (or, at $350k, about $2,917 a month of gross rent). A zero rent leaves GRM blank; a zero target leaves the reverse fields blank.

When investors use GRM

Buyers use GRM to compare listings on an apples-to-apples gross-rent basis before they dig into expenses. Brokers and listing sheets often quote it as a shorthand for price relative to rent. Owners recompute it when rents change after a lease renewal or a vacancy fill. It is a screen, not a full underwrite: two properties with the same GRM can still diverge once vacancy, operating costs, deferred maintenance and financing enter the picture.

FAQ

What is gross rent multiplier (GRM)?

GRM is the purchase price divided by the property’s annual gross rent (before vacancy, expenses or debt). A $350,000 property collecting $2,450 a month ($29,400 a year) has a GRM of about 11.90. Lower GRMs mean more rent relative to price; they can also mark higher-expense or softer markets. GRM ignores operating costs.

How is GRM different from cap rate?

Cap rate uses net operating income (after vacancy and operating expenses). GRM uses gross rent only. Two buildings with the same GRM can have very different cap rates once expenses and vacancy differ. Use GRM as a quick screen; use cap rate (and cash flow) when you have a rent roll and expense history.

Should I use monthly or annual rent?

Either works if you are consistent. This calculator takes monthly gross rent and multiplies by 12 for the annual figure used in GRM. Use scheduled/contract rent for a screen, or trailing collected rent if you want a more conservative number—just stay consistent across deals you compare.

What is a &ldquo;good&rdquo; GRM?

There is no universal number. GRMs vary by market, property type, unit mix and how rents are quoted. Compare a deal to similar local rentals rather than to a national rule of thumb. This calculator only does the arithmetic; it does not say whether a multiplier is attractive.

Does GRM include vacancy, expenses or the mortgage?

No. GRM is gross rent only: it ignores vacancy, operating expenses, CapEx, financing and taxes. For an unlevered income screen that uses NOI, use the cap rate calculator. For levered cash flow, use the cash flow or cash-on-cash calculators.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, and refreshing the page resets the defaults.
